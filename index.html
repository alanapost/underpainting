Underpainting

the plan underneath your plan

Just enough plan, just enough company, just enough guidance. For people making something alongside everything else they do.

An underpainting is the layer most people never see, and the part of the craft few are taught. Painters used to prepare it in company, grinding pigments and readying canvases side by side. Underpainting brings that workshop to your project.

Where things tend to stall

  • You know what you want to make, but not what goes into making it.
  • You have more ideas than time, and can’t tell which one matters most.
  • You keep meaning to update, post or fix things, and it keeps sliding to next week.

None of that is on you. You spent years getting good at your own area, not at how websites and apps get made, or at running a project on your own. AI tools and site builders can overpaint you into a corner, or leave you with expensive or hard-to-manage gaps and risks a human would have flagged. That’s where you need underpainting.

The layers you can’t see yet

You can picture the finished thing. What you can’t see yet is everything underneath it, the layers nobody tells you about. Where it lives online, what privacy rules apply, how payments work, what needs to be secure, what happens when something breaks. It’s hard to plan for things you don’t know exist.

Sometimes the gap is just a name. If you don’t know what you need is called a “digital asset management” system, you can’t look it up or compare options. Think of it as a reverse dictionary: you describe it, and find out what it’s called.

Underpainting helps you sketch those layers in first, break the work into pieces small enough to actually do, and spot when you need a specialist like a lawyer or accountant. Then you can focus on the painting.

How it works

Three layers, each resting on the one below.

A plan you can see

Your notes, ideas and half-finished pieces go into one private page: now, next, later, done. Whatever’s in “now” is what you pick up next. It’s yours to export any time.

A rhythm that keeps it moving

Every Wednesday, bring whatever’s in “now” to the work session. Everyone says what they’re working on, then works quietly side by side for an hour, like artists sharing a studio. It’s free with sign-up.

Sign up for the work session · Wednesdays, 10–11:30am Pacific (1–2:30pm Eastern)

Between sessions, the members’ chat is where you ask questions and show what you’ve made. It’s light on purpose: come when you can.

Someone who knows what goes underneath

Two 45-minute 1:1 sessions a month. You work out the point of what you’re doing, the smallest version that gets you there, and what you didn’t know you needed. Nobody does the work for you or runs your project, and you can bring whatever’s making the work hard. You make the calls.

Put together, it runs like this

  1. Pick the next step. In a 1:1, you work out what matters most right now and the smallest thing that moves it forward. It goes on your plan under “now”.
  2. Do it in company. Bring it to the Wednesday work session and work on it for an hour alongside other people.
  3. Ask when you’re stuck. Post in the chat or on your plan, and get an answer within two working days.
  4. Look back, then go again. At your next 1:1, you see what moved, adjust the plan and pick the next step.

Each round, the plan gets clearer and deciding what to do gets easier. On Community, you keep your own list and bring your next step to the session and the chat.

What changes

  • Not knowing what you don’t know→Seeing what’s underneath before you start
  • Ideas scattered across notes, tabs and your head→One plan you can open any time
  • Waiting until you feel ready→Taking the next step anyway
  • Busy, but not sure it’s going anywhere→Progress you can see

Who it’s for

People who are good at what they do and are making something alongside everything else: a website, a book, an app, a body of artwork, a newsletter, a shop. No technical background needed. It’s your painting, and you make the calls. Underpainting helps with what goes underneath.

It’s built for people whose focus doesn’t always cooperate. Maybe your brain works its own way, your life has changed shape, or you know how to do this and something is still holding it back. So it’s small steps, one page, and company while you work.

Questions

Will you build it for me?

That’s not what membership is. You stay in charge of your own work, and you get help to plan it, prioritise it and keep it moving. If there’s a specific job you’d rather hand off, ask for a quote.

Is this coaching?

Partly. You have goals and things you want to do toward them. You’re getting serious about doing them, and you keep running into problems along the way. The 1:1s help with that by weaving together light practices from art, product design, project management and research on focus and attention: whatever fits you and the work. The aim isn’t to keep you coming back forever. It’s to help you build the habits to steer your own work.

Why not just ask an AI?

Go ahead. An AI answers the question you ask. Underpainting helps you spot the questions you haven’t asked yet, decide what’s worth doing at all, and remember where you left off. You’ll also hear when an AI’s suggestion will cost you later.

I already use an app. Do I have to switch?

Keep it if it’s working. The plan here is for when it isn’t, or when you want a second pair of eyes on it too.

What if my project never really “finishes”?

Most don’t, and that’s fine. Painters rework a canvas for years, and websites, apps and projects keep going too. The goal is steady progress toward the results you want from it.

Who runs Underpainting?

Underpainting is run by one person, for now: someone who went to art school, started out in tech support, then spent years as a project, product and program manager. That meant turning fuzzy ideas into briefs at web agencies, working in heavily regulated fields like security and energy, and choosing tools at a large consumer app against legal, financial and security standards. They’re good at spotting the big gotchas early and at right-sizing things, so you don’t overbuild or paint yourself into a corner. They also have ADHD, so they know what it’s like to have solid ideas and a hard time keeping the work flowing. Art school is where the name comes from. The rest is how the plan gets made.
